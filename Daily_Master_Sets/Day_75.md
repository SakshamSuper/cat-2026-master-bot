# RC 1 — PRECISION AND MEANING

### Passage

Precision is often treated as evidence that uncertainty has been reduced. A statement containing several decimal places can appear more trustworthy than one expressed in approximate terms. Yet precision in a measurement does not necessarily imply precision in what the measurement means. The numerical procedure may be exact while the concept being measured remains open to interpretation.

This becomes especially important when a complex phenomenon is represented by a single indicator. Such compression can be useful: it allows large quantities of information to be compared quickly. But compression also requires selection. Some features become visible while others disappear from consideration. The resulting number may therefore be useful without being a complete representation.

The sensible response is not to reject numerical measures because they simplify reality. Instead, one should ask what the measure captures, what it leaves out, and whether that trade-off is appropriate for the question at hand. A precise measurement can be an excellent tool without being a complete account of the phenomenon.

# RC 2 — CHOICE ARCHITECTURE

### Passage

Choices rarely occur in a completely neutral environment. The way alternatives are displayed can affect which options attract attention, how much effort is required to select them, and how their consequences are understood. Recognising this influence does not mean that individuals cease to be responsible for their choices. It means that choice operates within a context.

Digital interfaces make this especially visible. One option may be placed prominently on a screen while another requires several additional steps. Both remain available, but they are not equally convenient. A user may therefore choose the prominent option without having been forced to do so. The environment has influenced the practical cost of the alternatives rather than eliminated the individual's freedom.

This distinction between formal freedom and practical accessibility is important. Formal freedom concerns whether a person is permitted to choose an option. Practical accessibility concerns how easy it is to pursue that option. A system can preserve the first while altering the second.

# 🎯 DAY 75 — 99+ PERCENTILE SHORTCUT BANK

### Arithmetic

For fractional journey distances:

\[
\boxed{\text{Choose total distance conveniently}}
\]

then calculate total time.

For work:

\[
\boxed{\text{LCM of completion times} \rightarrow \text{work units}}
\]

is usually faster than fractions.

### Algebra

If:

\[
x+\frac1x=a,
\]

immediately use:

\[
\boxed{x^3+\frac1{x^3}=a^3-3a}.
\]

For quadratic roots:

\[
\boxed{\alpha+\beta,\ \alpha\beta}
\]

are often enough; solving the quadratic is unnecessary.

---

### Geometry

For a chord of length \(c\):

\[
\boxed{r^2=d^2+\left(\frac c2\right)^2}.
\]

For two chords on opposite sides:

\[
\boxed{d_1+d_2=\text{distance between chords}}.
\]

Subtract the two equations to eliminate \(r^2\).

---

### Number System

For highest power of a composite number:

1. Factor the base.
2. Count each prime's exponent in \(n!\).
3. Divide by the exponent required in the base.
4. Take the minimum.

---

### Modern Math

For “exactly \(r\)”:

\[
\boxed{\binom nrp^rq^{n-r}}.
\]

For “at least \(r\)” in combinations, explicitly enumerate:

\[
r,r+1,\ldots
\]

unless complement counting is faster.

---

### 99+ Challenge

For:

\[
24\mid xyz,
\]

think:

\[
24=2^3\cdot3.
\]

The question is therefore not “which number is divisible by 24?” but:

> **Where can the three factors of 2 and one factor of 3 come from?**

That change in viewpoint is the difference between brute-force enumeration and a 99+ approach.
