# RC 1 — MEASUREMENT AND SIMPLIFICATION

### Passage

Measurement is often presented as though its principal virtue were objectivity. Once a phenomenon is translated into a number, it appears to acquire a stability that ordinary descriptions lack. Yet the act of measurement does not simply reveal a property waiting to be discovered; it also determines which aspect of a phenomenon will count as relevant.

This does not make measurement arbitrary. A well-designed measure can be remarkably consistent and can allow comparisons that would otherwise be difficult or impossible. The problem begins when consistency is mistaken for comprehensiveness. A measure may reliably capture one dimension while systematically excluding another.

Consequently, disputes about measurement are sometimes misdiagnosed as disputes about whether numbers are trustworthy. The more important question is often what the number has been designed to represent. A measurement can be perfectly dependable within its intended scope while becoming misleading when that scope is silently expanded.

# RC 2 — INCENTIVES AND SOCIAL NORMS

### Passage

An incentive is often imagined as a simple lever: alter the reward attached to an action and the frequency of that action should change correspondingly. Such a model is useful in some circumstances, but it becomes inadequate when behaviour carries meanings that are not reducible to material payoff.

Suppose a neighbourhood traditionally organises voluntary clean-up activities. If residents suddenly receive a small payment for participating, the number of participants may rise. But another possibility is that some residents begin to view participation as an economic transaction rather than as a civic obligation. The incentive may therefore increase participation while simultaneously weakening the norm that previously sustained it.

The lesson is not that incentives are harmful. Rather, their consequences may depend on what they interact with. Where behaviour is embedded in social expectations, changing the material reward can alter not only the immediate calculation faced by individuals but also the meaning they attach to the activity.
