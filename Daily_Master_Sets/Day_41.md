### Passage

Measurement is attractive partly because it converts complicated judgments into apparently precise numbers. A number allows an organisation to compare outcomes, establish targets and monitor changes over time. Yet precision should not be confused with completeness: a metric captures only those aspects of a phenomenon that its designers have chosen to measure.

The problem becomes more serious when rewards are attached to a metric. People respond to incentives, often quite rationally, by directing greater effort toward what is being rewarded. The resulting improvement in the metric may therefore coexist with deterioration in the broader objective.

This does not imply that measurement should be abandoned. Without indicators, many decisions would become difficult to evaluate. The more reasonable response is to treat indicators as imperfect representations of objectives, use multiple measures where appropriate and periodically examine whether the measurement system is producing unintended incentives.

### Passage

Technological innovation is often evaluated as though technical superiority alone determines success. But technologies do not operate in isolation. Their adoption depends on infrastructure, organisational capabilities, user habits, complementary technologies and economic incentives.

# 🔥 DAY 41 — 99+ SHORTCUT BANK

### Arithmetic
**Averages**

\[
\boxed{\text{Change in total}=n\times\text{change in average}}
\]

**Work**

Use LCM-based work units.

**Mixtures**

Track the component whose final quantity is easiest to determine.

### Algebra

If roots are in ratio \(m:n\), write them as:

\[
mx,nx.
\]

Don't use the quadratic formula.

For reciprocal powers:

\[
S_n=aS_{n-1}-S_{n-2},
\quad a=x+\frac1x.
\]

### Geometry

Similar triangles:

\[
\boxed{\text{Area ratio}=(\text{linear ratio})^2}
\]

Circle tangent:

\[
OP^2=OT^2+PT^2.
\]

### Number System

For huge powers:

1. Find the smallest useful cycle.
2. Reduce the exponent.
3. Calculate only the remaining power.

For divisibility:

\[
\boxed{\text{A but not B}=A-(A\cap B)}
\]

### P&C

When a divisibility rule fixes a digit, **fix that digit first**.

### Probability

Often:

\[
P(\text{different})=1-P(\text{same})
\]

is substantially faster.
