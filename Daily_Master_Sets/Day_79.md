# RC 1 — THE VALUE OF CLASSIFICATION

### Passage

Classification is frequently treated as a neutral activity: objects are placed into categories according to properties they already possess. Yet classification does more than record similarities. It establishes which similarities matter for a particular purpose and which differences can be ignored. The same object may therefore belong to different legitimate classifications depending on the question being asked.

This does not imply that categories are arbitrary. A useful classification must still respond to features that are relevant to the task at hand. But relevance itself is not a property that exists independently of purpose. A classification designed to predict behaviour may group objects differently from one designed to explain their historical origins.

The mistake, therefore, is not classification itself but the assumption that one classification necessarily reveals the only meaningful structure of the world. Categories are tools. Their value depends partly on what they are intended to accomplish.

# RC 2 — EFFICIENCY AND PURPOSE

### Passage

Efficiency is often praised as though it were an independent virtue. An efficient process uses fewer resources, takes less time or produces more output for a given input. Yet efficiency has meaning only relative to an objective. A process may be extraordinarily efficient at producing an outcome that nobody actually wants.

This becomes especially significant when organisations optimise measurable targets. Once a target is made explicit, employees can often discover ways of improving the measured result without necessarily improving the broader objective from which the target was derived. The resulting behaviour may look irrational only if one forgets that people are responding to the incentive structure actually presented to them.

The solution is not to reject efficiency or measurement. Rather, organisations must distinguish between improving a metric and improving the purpose that the metric was intended to represent. Efficiency is valuable, but only when the thing being made efficient is itself worth pursuing.
