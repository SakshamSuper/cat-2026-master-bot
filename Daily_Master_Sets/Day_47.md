Let original price be ₹100 and original consumption be 100 units.

Original expenditure:

\[
100\times100=10000.
\]

New price:

\[
100(1.25)=125.
\]

New expenditure must be 110% of original:

\[
11000.
\]

Therefore new consumption:

\[
\frac{11000}{125}=88.
\]

Reduction:

\[
100-88=12.
\]

Hence percentage reduction:

\[
\boxed{12\%}.
\]

### Correction
The correct answer is **12%**, which is **option B**, not C.

\[
\boxed B
\]

**Source:** CAT-level inspired  
**Topic:** Percentages  
**Difficulty:** 4/5  
**Recommended time:** 1.5 min  

**Shortcut:**

\[
\text{Price increase}=25\%,\quad
\text{expenditure increase}=10\%.
\]

Required consumption factor:

\[
\frac{1.10}{1.25}=0.88.
\]

\[
12\%.

## Q4 — **C**

\[
\frac{x-2}{x+1}\ge0.
\]

Critical points:

\[
x=2,\quad x=-1.
\]

Sign analysis:

- \(x<-1\): positive
- \(-1<x<2\): negative

At \(x=-1\), expression is undefined, so -1 is excluded.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{x<-1\text{ or }x\ge2}.
\]

\[
\boxed C

# Q12 — **40**

We need:

\[
n^2-10n+21
\]

to be a perfect square.

Complete the square:

\[
n^2-10n+21
=
(n-5)^2-4.
\]

Let:

\[
(n-5)^2-4=k^2.
\]

Thus:

\[
(n-5)^2-k^2=4.
\]

Factor:

\[
(n-5-k)(n-5+k)=4.
\]

Both factors have the same parity.

Positive factor pairs of 4:

\[
(2,2),\quad(1,4)
\]

but 1 and 4 have different parity, so only:

\[
2,2
\]

works.

Thus:

\[
n-5-k=2
\]

and

\[
n-5+k=2.
\]

Therefore:

\[
n=7,\quad k=0.
\]

But because factors can both be negative:

\[
(-2,-2)
\]

also gives:

\[
n=3.
\]

Thus possible \(n\) are:

\[
3,7.
\]

Their sum:

\[
\boxed{10}.
\]

### Correction

The correct answer is **10**.

There is no answer option because Q12 is TITA, so it remains valid.

**Topic:** Algebra + Number System  
**Difficulty:** 5/5  
**Recommended time:** 3 min
