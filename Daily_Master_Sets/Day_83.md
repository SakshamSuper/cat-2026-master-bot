# RC 1 — REPRESENTATION AND PURPOSE

### Passage

A representation is often evaluated according to how faithfully it resembles what it represents. Yet resemblance alone cannot determine whether a representation is useful. A subway map, for instance, may distort geographical distances while accurately preserving the relationships that matter to a passenger trying to move between stations. Its departures from physical geography are therefore not necessarily defects; they may be precisely what allows the map to perform its intended function.

This suggests that representations should be understood as selective rather than comprehensive. They highlight certain relationships while suppressing others. What deserves emphasis depends partly on the task for which the representation is constructed. A geological map and a transit map of the same city need not privilege the same features because their users have different questions.

This does not imply that representations can be judged by any standard whatsoever. A transit map that places stations in the wrong order would be defective for navigation, even if its visual design were elegant. Selectivity is useful only when it preserves the relationships relevant to the purpose of the representation.

# RC 2 — METRICS AND INCENTIVES

### Passage

Organisations frequently transform broad objectives into measurable indicators because indicators make performance easier to compare and monitor. Yet measurement creates a subtle risk. Once an indicator is attached to rewards, people may rationally devote effort to improving the indicator itself, even when the improvement has little effect on the broader objective that the indicator was intended to represent.

This does not necessarily reflect a failure of motivation. An employee responding to the incentives established by an organisation may be behaving exactly as those incentives encourage. The difficulty emerges when the organisation treats success on the indicator as if it were identical to success on the objective.

Consequently, metrics should be regarded as instruments rather than complete definitions of organisational success. Their value depends on the continued relationship between the number being measured and the purpose for which it was introduced. If that relationship weakens, impressive performance on the metric can coexist with little meaningful progress.

\boxed{972}.

# 🔥 DAY 83 — 99+ PERCENTILE SHORTCUT BANK

### Arithmetic

**Time & Work**

When two people have known individual/combined times, use an LCM work unit.

\[
\boxed{\text{Rate}=\frac{\text{work}}{\text{time}}}
\]

**Savings**

Always establish:

\[
\boxed{\text{Saving}=\text{Income}-\text{Expenditure}}
\]

before applying percentage changes.

**Weighted average**

If the mean is between two group averages:

\[
\boxed{\text{Group ratio}=
\frac{\text{distance of other group from mean}}
{\text{distance of this group from mean}}}
\]

### Algebra

For roots \(x,y\):

\[
\boxed{x+y=-b/a,\qquad xy=c/a}.
\]

Whenever the question asks for symmetric expressions, **do not solve the quadratic**.

For example:

\[
\boxed{(x-y)^2=2(x^2+y^2)-(x+y)^2}.
\]

### Geometry

Recognise classic triples:

\[
\boxed{5-12-13}
\]

before doing full calculations.

For a trapezium:

\[
\boxed{\text{Area}=
\frac{\text{sum of parallel sides}}2\times h}.
\]

### Number System

Before finding a power cycle, reduce the base:

\[
11\equiv3\pmod8.
\]

Then:

\[
3^2\equiv1\pmod8.
\]

For square divisors:

\[
\boxed{\text{only even prime exponents are allowed}}.
\]

### Modern Math

Circular arrangements:

\[
\boxed{(n-1)!}.
\]

For “not adjacent”:

\[
\boxed{\text{Total}-\text{adjacent}}.
\]

For probability “at least \(k\)”:

\[
\boxed{\text{sum all cases }\ge k}
\]

or use the complement if shorter.
