## Q1 — **C, 6 days**

A's rate:

\[
\frac1{18}
\]

B's rate:

\[
\frac1{27}.
\]

Together:

\[
\frac1{18}+\frac1{27}
=
\frac5{54}.
\]

B works alone for 12 days:

\[
12\left(\frac1{27}\right)=\frac49.
\]

Therefore the work completed by A+B together is:

\[
1-\frac49=\frac59.
\]

If they work together for \(t\) days:

\[
t\left(\frac5{54}\right)=\frac59.
\]

Hence:

\[
t=\frac59\cdot\frac{54}{5}=6.
\]

\[
\boxed C
\]

**Source:** CAT-level inspired  
**Topic:** Time & Work  
**Difficulty:** 4/5  
**Recommended time:** 1.5 min  

**Shortcut:** First calculate the portion B completes in 12 days:

\[
12/27=4/9.
\]

The remaining \(5/9\) was completed jointly.

**Common trap:** Adding 12 to the required joint time before finding the work split.

**Key concept:** Convert workers into rates, not days.

**Alternate:** Assume total work = LCM(18,27)=54 units.

# Q12 — **3125**

We have:

\[
x+y=50
\]

and:

\[
xy=m^2.
\]

Then \(x,y\) are roots of:

\[
t^2-50t+m^2=0.
\]

For integer \(x,y\), the discriminant must be a perfect square:

\[
2500-4m^2=d^2.
\]

Since \(d=|x-y|\) is even, write:

\[
d=2k.
\]

Then:

\[
2500-4m^2=4k^2
\]

\[
625=m^2+k^2.
\]

So we need Pythagorean triples with hypotenuse 25.

The relevant triples are:

\[
7-24-25,
\]

\[
15-20-25,
\]

and the degenerate:

\[
0-25-25.
\]

Thus:

\[
m=7,15,20,24,25.
\]

Hence possible products are:

\[
49,\ 225,\ 400,\ 576,\ 625.
\]

For the first four, \(x\neq y\), so each product occurs for two ordered pairs.

For \(m=25\):

\[
x=y=25,
\]

so it occurs only once.

Therefore:

\[
2(49+225+400+576)+625.
\]

\[
=2(1250)+625
\]

\[
=\boxed{3125}.
\]

**Source:** CAT-level inspired  
**Topic:** Number theory / perfect squares  
**Difficulty:** 5/5  
**Recommended time:** 4 min  

**Time-saving shortcut:** Convert the condition \(xy\) perfect square into a discriminant condition.

\[
\boxed{(x-y)^2+4xy=(x+y)^2}.

\[
(x-y)^2+(2m)^2=50^2.
\]

This immediately creates a Pythagorean-triple problem.

**Common trap:** Finding the possible products but forgetting that asymmetric pairs occur twice.

**Key concept revision:**

\[
x+y=S,\quad xy=P
\]

means \(x,y\) are roots of:

\[
t^2-St+P=0.
\]

**Alternate method:** Search factor pairs of 50 with equal sum 50 and test whether their product is square.
